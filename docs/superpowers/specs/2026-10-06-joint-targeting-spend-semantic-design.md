## 3. Platform semantic invariants

1. Each assertion has one semantic owner and explicit meaning/scope. Domain consumers cannot edit/redefine declarations, observation history or shared interpretation rules.
2. Derived claims remain distinguishable from premises, preserve derivation lineage and cannot assert stronger evidence than premises support. Interpretation is not verification.
3. Domain sufficiency can be stricter than shared usability, never weaker. Synthesis cannot repair missing evidence with wording, averaging or scores.
4. A decision is an identifiable, versioned governed conclusion about a bounded question with reasons, limits and contributions; prose is its projection.
5. Provider/domain count, sector, country list and first behavior do not define platform meaning.
6. Historical meaning is stable. Material new inputs/policy/restatements justify a new decision version, never silent historical mutation.
7. Optional Intent stays optional. Withhold unavailable intent-dependent implications without universally blocking unrelated reasoning.
8. Unsupported, insufficient and conflicting propositions remain distinct. Withholding is valid; universal withholding does not prove this substantive Slice.
9. Decision, action, approval/authority and execution have separate owners. Decision grants no write authority.
10. Extension needs established meaning, consumer and evidence; no speculative infrastructure is needed to preserve these obligations.
