## Case A — cheaper inquiries versus qualified clients and follow-up

Reused research: BF-03 v1.1 I-07 qualification/handoff, interfaces/reverse effects (delayed follow-up, capacity, economic corrections), practice/experiment comparison; BF-04 v1.1 opening decision, explicit unresolved ownership, CRM Closed-Won distinction and business-model examples; accepted CAEI v1.1 §8.2–8.3 assessment/selection and §13 native recommendations. Exact paths/hashes in evidence. These studies are research/design evidence, not independently replicated causal results.

Question: should more money go to a channel with cheaper platform leads when accepted inquiries, response time, sales maturity or capacity suggest another problem?

Required distinctions: platform conversion, accepted inquiry, CRM acceptance, fulfilment, payment and corrected economic outcome; cohort/denominator, event time/maturity, scoped lawful identity linkage, qualification-criteria version, source coverage and economics. Missing response data does not establish slow response; Closed-Won does not establish cash or profit.

Competing explanations: poor lead fit, delayed response, misleading measurement, capacity limits, price/offer friction and channel effects. Alternatives: request evidence, test response/offer, retain allocation, or justify a channel change. A native recommendation is an evaluation input, never execution authority. Practitioner advice also requires qualification and counterevidence.

Integration result: current D2 offer/interest methods cannot compute this diagnosis. Shared provenance/qualification patterns do not imply common numeric meaning. The chosen diagnostic behavior's owner, inputs and accepted outcome need explicit resolution using existing domain research. Do not create a global score, confidence average, CRM, registry or duplicate economic truth store.

Future discriminating tests — NOT EXECUTED:

- Same CPL, different accepted-client cohorts: do not equate cheaper leads with better outcome.
- Delayed response: support investigation without claiming causal loss from correlation alone.
- Missing denominator, immature sales or absent capacity evidence: limit dependent claims explicitly.
- Revoked identity-link rights: prevent the dependent inference.
- Later refund: revise economic assessment without rewriting the original inquiry.
- Diagnostic recommendation: cannot authorize spend changes or send follow-up.

## Case B — opportunity investigation without an ad account or declared goal

Reused research: Blueprint v1.1 §11.1–11.3 (reuse and science-to-opportunity direction), CAEI v1.1 §8 (claim-scoped methods/evidence); actual offer/interest algorithms and their limits.

Question: what deserves investigation from changed interest or comparable offers before a campaign or stated advertiser goal exists?

Offer equivalence and temporal interest change have different meanings; neither substitutes for the other. Geography, product/service identity, issuer-local subject keys, comparison basis, time, scale and source rights matter. Interest supports investigating reach, not buyer counts or profitable demand. Comparable terms can support a later price comparison, not commercial superiority.

Integration result: existing pure methods accept these bounded inputs without an ad account/goal. Actual application use still needs qualified inputs/current D3 rights.
